I study how social experiences and the brain’s functional organization relate to cognition across the lifespan, from childhood development to aging and neurodegenerative diseases.

Research interests

  • Social Neuroscience
  • Developmental Neuroscience
  • Cognitive Reserve
  • Brain Aging & Alzheimer’s
  • Multimodal Neuroimaging

What first fascinated me about sociology was how our thoughts and behaviors are shaped not only by individual characteristics, but also by the relationships and environments around us. As I studied social networks, I became increasingly curious about how such relationships and experiences influence individuals. This led me to a broader question: why do people think and respond differently, even when they are in similar environments?

That curiosity prompted me to expand my research into neuroscience. While sociology taught me to understand people within their social contexts, neuroscience offered new ways to investigate the neural processes associated with these experiences and individual differences. I see the two fields not as separate paths, but as complementary perspectives that together offer a more complete understanding of human behavior.

By combining large-scale neuroimaging cohort analyses with behavioral and fMRI experiments, I aim to connect different types of data and disciplinary perspectives to explore relationships that might be difficult to uncover through any single approach.
