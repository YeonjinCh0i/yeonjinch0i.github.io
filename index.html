What I’m working on now

Current projects at the Brain Development Imaging Lab, Korea Brain Research Institute.

I study how the brain reads emotion in faces, and how genes and social relationships feed into those responses. Right now my work spans how children and older adults process facial emotion, and how to measure social cognition outside the lab.

Dataset

KOFED

Building the Korean Older Adult Facial Expression Database — a resource for studying how aging shapes emotional expression and perception.

Tool

MoodSpy

Developing a digital tool for measuring social cognition, bringing emotion and face-reading tasks into everyday, scalable settings.

Question

Faces & the developing brain

How limbic–cortical integration shapes the efficiency of reading neutral and negative faces in children — and how OXTR and social networks relate to it.

About

My work sits between social science and neuroscience. Trained first in sociology at Yonsei and now in bio and brain engineering at KAIST, I combine socio-centric network data, genetics, and neuroimaging to ask how our social worlds get under the skin and into the brain — from adolescence through later life.
